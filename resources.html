Help & Support

Asking for help is a strong move, not a weak one. Here are answers and people to talk to.

Frequently asked questions

Tap a question to open the answer.

I feel anxious about school. Who can I talk to?

Start with someone you trust: a parent or guardian, your class adviser, a favorite teacher or the guidance counselor. You do not need the perfect words. "School has been hard for me lately" is enough to begin.

How many absences is too many?

There is no single number that fits everyone. What matters is the pattern: are absences becoming regular, or is learning starting to suffer? To find out about the school's attendance policy, ask your class adviser or the guidance office.

What should I do if I am sick and cannot come to school?

Rest, and have a parent or guardian tell the school as early as possible. When you feel better, ask a classmate or teacher what you missed and make a plan to catch up.

I missed a lot of lessons. How do I catch up?

Start small. List what you missed, ask your teachers which topics matter most and pick one to work on each day. Borrow notes from a classmate you trust, and tell your teachers you are working on it.

Will I get in trouble for asking for help?

Asking for help is exactly what we want you to do. The goal is to understand what is going on and support you, not to punish you.

My friend keeps missing school. What can I do?

Check in kindly, invite them to walk to class with you and share notes. If you are worried about their safety or wellbeing, tell a trusted adult. See the Upstanders page for more ideas.

As a parent, what should I do if my child refuses to go to school?

Stay calm and ask open questions. Try to find out whether something is happening, such as worry, bullying, a hard subject or trouble with friends. Then contact the class adviser or guidance office so you can make a plan together.

What if something is wrong at home and it is making me miss school?

You deserve support. Talk to the guidance counselor or a teacher you trust. They can help you find options and keep your learning on track. If you are ever unsafe, tell an adult immediately.

Where to get help

  • School guidance counselor: a private, caring space to talk about worries, stress or problems at school or home.
  • Your class adviser or a teacher you trust: they can help with catch-up work and connect you with other support.
  • A parent or guardian: talking at home is often the first step.
  • A trusted friend sometimes just talking it out with a trusted friend helps
  • Emergency services If you have absolutely no one, call emergency services

If someone is in danger right now, tell an adult immediately and call your local emergency number.

Not sure where to begin?

Pick the person you feel most comfortable with and say one honest sentence. They can help from there.
